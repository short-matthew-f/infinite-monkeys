Bureau Art Directions

Three ways to make the Bureau itself the interface: rooms you tap instead of menus, in a 2.5D paper-cutout style. All three show the same scene with mock data. Tap around inside the phone: open Personnel, press Hire, dismiss Ernest, try the directory.

Opus summary

What the panel agreed on

  1. None of them is roomy yet, and that's the brief's fault. It asked for eight monkeys, four rooms, a header and Ernest in one phone screen. The fix is the same for all three: show less at a time (one room or about 1.5 floors), make monkeys 50–60 px tall instead of about 30, and let scrolling, panning or dollying reveal the rest.
  2. Ernest is too big. In every version his card covers a third of the floor. He should peek in from a corner, point at what he's explaining, and never block the thing he's teaching.
  3. Variety reads as accessories, not personalities. At phone size the behaviours are too small to see. Bigger monkeys, distinct silhouettes (size, ears, posture, profile), and more exaggerated poses.
  4. The paper depth is subtle in stills. They read as clean vector with drop shadows. Visible paper rims, a second shadow tier and more value separation between layers would sell the cutout look.
  5. The room sheets are the strongest part of all three. Numbered forms, the curved tangerine Hire button, and the camera moving to the room before the sheet arrives all landed well.

My recommendation

Pop-up Floor Plan as the base: it scored best on whimsy and readability, and it's the closest to the Egg Inc map you described. Take the Dollhouse's vertical stack for growth: as the Bureau gains floors, you scroll up the building, and each floor is its own roomy pop-up plan. That also gives the infinity ceremony its climb. The Theater's sideways dolly fits the Hotel's endless corridor better than the finite climb, so I'd keep it in mind for the Hotel phase.

Round 2 would rebuild the winner with the fixes above, and the panel reviews it again.

Evidence: scripted Chrome screenshots at 390 × 844 (light, dark, reduced motion, animation frames, taps), computed contrast, and static inspection. Not yet tested on a physical phone or by players. Reviewers: a Sonnet agent for taste and a Haiku agent for the checklist on each approach.
