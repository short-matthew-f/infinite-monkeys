# Pop-up Floor Plan: two zoom levels

**Idea.** One floor, two camera levels. Play view is roomy (round 2's feel, all four areas still in the world). Floor-plan view pulls back to the whole floor with big labels. Materials, parts kit and sheets are round 3's; only the layout, camera and the six shared fixes changed.

**World.** 780 x 1530 px (was 780 x 1250). Departments and the Director stay at the top either side of the water-cooler nook; the Typing Pool moved down 120 px; Personnel moved down 225 px and sits on the pool's centre line, joined to it by a carpet runner (another runner links the nook to the pool). Between every pair of areas there is 150 to 200 px of open floor: runners, the mail-cart lane, aisle props. The entrance (door, mat, umbrella stand, waiting candidate, reception bench) is a strip below Personnel. The shredder station moved right so it does not get cut in the default frame. The mail-cart route, creases, walk path and culling boxes were re-measured for the new layout.

**Play view (default).** Zoom 1.04, so the desk monkeys read 55 to 64 px. Each area has a camera spot that frames the whole area with floor around it (a pool is 354 px wide on a 390 px screen). First load centres Personnel (desk, door, waiting candidate) with the Typing Pool's bottom row peeking in at the top. The camera is anchored so Ernest's card sits on empty floor below the pad, never on the candidate. The paper hand now hangs over the waiting candidate by the door, not over the sign. Pan still drags with momentum and is clamped. The Personnel hot area includes the door and candidate.

**Floor-plan view.** Zoom about 0.45 (computed from the viewport): every area is fully visible, monkeys are about 25 px. Room labels are real-size paper tags (number disc plus name, 44 px tall, with a lifted dog-ear) that sit on top of each pad like folder tabs; Personnel's tag carries a tangerine "Start here" flag until the first hire. They are counter-scaled in CSS (`scale(1 / var(--s))`), so they stay the same size at any zoom and fade in as you pull back. Dashed rings mark each room. Tapping a room (the area, its tag, or its button) flies the camera to play view framed on that room; a second tap opens its sheet. Ernest steps out of the way in this view and returns on Back.

**Switching.** The small paper "Floor plan" control sits top-left under the header (grid icon). In floor-plan view the same control becomes "Back" and drops to a bottom bar beside a one-line caption ("Tap a room to go there. Pinch in or tap Back to return."), so it never covers a room label. Two-finger pinch works continuously: the camera follows the fingers (zoom is clamped just past each level), and on release it snaps to the nearer level. Pinching in from the floor plan lands on the room nearest the fingers. Ctrl + wheel and trackpad pinch do the same. Escape in floor-plan view goes back. The Directory is unchanged and is the non-spatial list; a keyboard user who tabs to an area that is out of view gets the camera flown to it.

**Zoom transition.** The camera is three registered CSS numbers (`--tx`, `--ty`, `--s`) that ease together over 860 ms, so labels and the world stay in step. Reduced motion: the camera snaps behind a 140 ms fade (the `.fade` class), no easing, no travel; the tags appear with the snap.

**Culling.** Unchanged in method: ten `.area` groups, the camera rectangle is computed from the camera target in world coordinates, so it works in play view, floor-plan view and mid-pinch (areas are measured by their real rendered scale). Pinching out wakes everything; flying into play view pauses the areas that leave the frame.

**Shared fixes.**
1. Ernest docked keeps his face and pencil: a narrow card with just the head and pencil peeking over its top-right corner (the figure crops to the head with the SVG viewBox). The reel line is a single line (`nowrap`, 142 of 142 px measured, card widened to 168 px); the docked card hides the reel line by design.
2. Editor: stocky and hunched (width 52, head 16, no neck), dark green shirt, mustard braces, green eyeshade, round chair; no white shirt, no red tie, no headphones. Desk 1 is the tall white-shirt, red-tie, headphones one.
3. Sheet focus: opening moves focus to the sheet heading, Tab and Shift+Tab cycle inside the sheet (plus Ernest's button while he is docked beside it), focus that escapes is pulled back, Escape or Fold down returns focus to the area's button. `aria-modal` is true.
4. No colour emoji: the Departments buy buttons show a drawn paper banana plus the number, with the accessible name "Upgrade Recruiting: 90 bananas".
5. White shirts have a dark cut line and a shaded fill (sleeves get an outline too). Each pad now sits on an apron (a mid-value mat with a stitched edge), so the floor, apron and pad are three clear steps in both themes.
6. Control edges: new tokens `--ctl` (walnut on cream, a brass mix in dark) and `--ctl-hdr` (mustard in light, cream in dark). Computed against their backgrounds: Directory border 3.5:1 (light) and 3.7:1 (dark); button, Fold down, Understood and Hire edges 7.0:1 and 5.1:1 on the sheet; Floor plan and room tags 3.2 to 7.0:1 (light) and 3.9 to 6.3:1 (dark) on floor, apron and pad.

**Counts (scripted, headless Chrome, 390 x 844).** DOM 1,823 nodes at rest, 1,873 with a sheet open (round 3: 1,782 and 1,821). Running CSS animations: 52 in the default view (17 paused), 68 in floor-plan view (all areas awake), 50 to 52 in the other play frames, 0 when the page is hidden and 0 under reduced motion. Round 3 ran 65 in its default view.

**Evidence.** Computed contrast, scripted browser runs (synthetic two-pointer pinch, keyboard focus and Tab cycle, Escape, a full hire walk, reduced motion) and screenshots in `shots/`. Not tested on a device; the pinch uses pointer events, and the zoom easing uses `@property`, which needs Safari 16.4 or later.

**Known gaps.** The hand points at the candidate beside the door, not the door itself (the door sits under the pad corner). While Ernest is full-size, the reception bench and shredder station are behind his card. The second tap that opens a sheet is any tap on the framed area, not specifically the area that was framed. The floor-plan caption does not show on the first load (it appears only in that view), so pinch is discoverable from the caption and the control only. Sheet contents are still mock.
